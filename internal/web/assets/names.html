This demonstration lists {{.Target}} and nothing else. The hosts it may touch are compiled into the binary, so the field is fixed rather than open: a page that took any domain and then refused it would be advertising something this deployment does not do. Run the tool yourself to list your own. No name is invented. The names come from a public register of certificates, from the records this domain publishes, and from the certificates its own hosts present. What is asked, who is asked, and what it cannot show is written out in full.

{{else}}

A bare domain. No name is invented and nothing is sent to your hosts: the names come from a public register of certificates, published by whoever obtained them, and from the mail, sender policy and delegation records your domain publishes for anybody to read. Where this installation can be reached by anybody but you, it produces an inventory only for a domain you have proven control of — it lists an estate rather than measuring a host, and the party being listed cannot see it happen. What is asked, and what it cannot show is written out in full.

Optional. Read for the names their reverse records answer to, which finds a host from the other direction: one in no certificate, in no record this domain publishes, and in no register. Nothing is sent to the addresses themselves. A range wider than /20 (/116 for IPv6), or more than 4,096 addresses across all of them, is refused rather than cut short. What is asked, and the limits on a range.

Optional. One to a line, or separated by commas. They are taken as given — nothing here invents a name — then resolved and reported beside what the sources found, so a host of yours that no public source names is a row you can see. Up to 1,000 names; a longer list is a wordlist rather than an estate, and this mode does not try wordlists. What is asked, and why a list is not a wordlist.
