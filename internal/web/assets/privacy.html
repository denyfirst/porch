What is kept

  • Counts. Per scan, one number goes up, and one of strong, weak, insecure, or ungraded (ungraded means nothing was measured rather than nothing was wrong). Each check keeps its own block of these, naming the rules that graded them. There is also the day’s total, its date so a restart knows whether it is still today, and when counting began.
  • Refusals. How many requests were turned away and for which reason. The reasons name causes, never people.
  • The latest report of each check for each of our hosts, in memory: for a minute, or fifteen for the mail and DNS checks, which ask other people’s servers. And the certificate transparency monitor’s last answer about our domain, for half an hour, or for up to a day while the monitor is not answering. None of it is written to disk or says who asked.
  • Your address, in memory, for the rate limit. It is dropped about three minutes after your last request. For IPv6 the limit applies to your network block.

That is the whole record. The counts are public at /api/v1/stats and change at most once a minute, so they cannot be used to time a scan.

The hostname you ask about is sent in the request body, never in the URL. If a mail address is typed, the part before the @ is dropped. A host is refused only after it has been checked at least eight times in a few minutes, so a refusal tells you little about anyone else.

No cookies. Your browser stores only your light or dark choice. Pages load nothing from other sites: no analytics, no external fonts, no CDN. The clipboard is written in one place: the Copy button next to an install command. A report is never put there.

Logs

There is no record of what was scanned, by whom, or when. The service logs only that it started, that it stopped, and a failure to save the counts. The operating system keeps its usual firewall and login logs, and logs packets with forged source addresses.

The server is rented from Hetzner Online GmbH in Germany. Like any network provider, they can see that connections are made.

What a scan does

This deployment scans only the hosts this project owns. The list is built into the program. The transport and web checks run at most once a minute for each host, the mail and DNS checks at most once every fifteen minutes, and the certificate transparency monitor is asked at most once every half hour. Everyone in between gets the kept report, which says when it was made.

  • Transport: TLS handshakes, each closed when the handshake ends.
  • Reach: GET / over HTTPS and HTTP, and /.well-known/security.txt.
  • Mail: DNS lookups, the MTA-STS policy file, and a STARTTLS check on port 25. No mail is sent.
  • DNS: lookups, and questions to our name servers. A zone transfer is asked for and not read.

No logins, no exploits, no malformed packets, and no private addresses. Every connection resolves the name, checks the address, and connects to that address rather than to the name, so the resolver is asked many times.

Others are asked only about us: our certificate authority for its revocation list (no certificate authority is asked about one certificate), Cert Spotter for our certificates (crt.sh when Cert Spotter does not answer), our mail provider on port 25, and the .dev registry for our name servers.

Stopping a scan

Porch is open source, and anyone can run a copy. Our own scans come from addresses whose reverse DNS is porch.denyfirst.dev, and they reach only our own hosts. A scan from any other address was made by someone else’s copy: contact whoever is responsible for that address. What each check sends is in docs/checks.md.

To have your domain excluded from future releases, write to abuse@denyfirst.dev from an address at that domain. We reply within two working days.

What is not promised

A report describes one moment and is not an audit or a certification. It is offered as it is, without warranty of any kind.
