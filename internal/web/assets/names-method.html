The name inventory.

Lists the names under a domain, and says where each one was found. It grades nothing.

Where the names come from

  • Public certificates, from a certificate transparency monitor (crt.sh or SSLMate).
  • Your own DNS records: MX, SPF and NS.
  • A passive DNS register, only if the operator set one up with their own account. It can see behind wildcard certificates, but may hold typos and old names.
  • The hosts’ own certificates, if enabled: one handshake per name, closed when the certificate is read. This finds names from private certificate authorities.
  • Reverse DNS of an address range you name, on your own copy only. A range wider than a /20 (a /116 for IPv6), or ranges with more than 4,096 addresses in total, are refused.
  • Names you already have, one per line or separated by commas, or from a text file. Blank lines and lines beginning with # are skipped. The file is never uploaded as a file, but its names leave the page in the same request as the domain. Up to 1,000 names.

Each name says which source found it, and each source says what it found or why it found nothing. An empty source is never shown as an empty domain.

What is sent to your domain

Only lookups of its MX, SPF and NS records, through the resolver. No name is guessed and no wordlist is used.

Who is asked

The monitor learns that somebody is looking at your domain. That is why an inventory is made only for a domain proven to the copy making it, including the command line and a copy only you can reach.

What it cannot show

This is not a list of all your hosts. It misses:

  • hosts with no public certificate;
  • hosts behind a wildcard certificate, unless a register was read;
  • names missing from the monitor’s history;
  • hosts your DNS has no reason to name.

The dates show when each name was covered by a certificate. A name whose last certificate expired long ago is either gone or now behind a wildcard.
